Welcome. This report reviews three months of GA4 data for the Google Merchandise Store demo account, 1 October to 31 December 2025, for our ecommerce team: manager, creative content, advertising, ecommerce product and analytics. This landing dashboard is the executive summary: the whole quarter on one screen. GA4-reported: total revenue $955,132.46; 161,200 active users; 271,641 sessions; engagement rate 59.38%. Calculated: revenue per session $3.52 for the quarter, and revenue per active user $5.93. The purchase rate is 3.29%: 4,756 users reached purchase out of 144,649 users at session start in the closed Purchase journey funnel. It is a funnel rate, not a site-wide conversion rate. Trend: revenue per session rose from $3.17 in October to $3.28 in November and $3.97 in December, a 21% rise from November to December, while December sessions fell 21.3% and revenue only 4.7%. The bottom row is what to watch: mobile is 34.29% of active users but 5.68% of revenue; 9.81% of sessions are Unassigned; Email earns $5.49 per session against the $3.52 average. Next quarter, in priority order: tag every campaign link, fix the mobile payment step, remarket to cart abandoners. Revenue per session is my calculation and is not return on ad spend.

04 / 21

Where two GA4 reports disagree, I flagged the conflict rather than picking the convenient number, and used each figure only for what it can support. One: Oct + Nov + Dec sessions add to 277,373, but the quarter report shows 271,641, 2.1% fewer, because GA4 estimates session counts; revenue matches exactly, so quarter totals are used for totals and months only for trends. Two: the Purchase journey shows 8,412 users beginning checkout and 4,756 purchasing, while the Checkout journey shows 9,973 and 5,020. Both are closed funnels; the Purchase journey only counts users at begin checkout if they first passed session start, view product and add to cart, while the Checkout journey starts at begin checkout itself. The two are kept separate. Three: item revenue is $977,310.64 against total revenue of $955,132.46, so item revenue is used only to compare products. Four: some items have more add-to-carts than views, for example the Android Classic Plushie with 15,678 adds and 2,208 views; treated as a tracking anomaly to verify. Five: gender and age are unknown for 63.34% of active users, so demographics are indicative only. Six: the 318,344 key events include all key events, not just purchases, so they are not used as a purchase conversion rate.

Content here means the checkout and product-page experience. Finding: mobile payment-step drop 44.4% against 19.0% on desktop; the New York Heavyweight Pullover converts 9.4% and the Super G Sporty Zip 8.6% of views into add-to-carts, against 40.3% for the Dino sweater.
